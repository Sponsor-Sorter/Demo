# Meadow Market Littles: game ideas

Designed with Dot for children ages 3–6. The concepts use picture-led taps, optional narration, sound-off play, gentle feedback, and no child accounts, chat, or punitive timers.

## Added: Story Wagon

Two players take turns choosing a farm friend, a place, and a fun activity. They make three short stories per round, then swap who starts the next story. Every picture choice is accepted; players get a small visual celebration when a story is complete. This adds open-ended shared storytelling to the existing co-op collection.

## Best next game: Kindness Meadow

A farm friend is waiting, needs company, or has dropped a toy. One player picks the picture moment; the other chooses a kind response such as waving, sharing, or inviting a friend to play. Every response moves the story forward, with no wrong answer. This adds gentle social-emotional play without copying the care game’s pet routine.

## Other candidates

- **Barnyard Action Parade:** players take turns choosing big movement or animal-action pictures—flap, stretch, stomp, wave—and watch the farm friends act them out. Keep it as a visual pretend-play activity so children do not need to move around the device. It should have its own animation loop to stay distinct from Barnyard Band.
- **Farm Shape Studio:** one child picks a simple background stamp and the other places a friend or crop, then the roles swap. This can be a co-op mode for creative play, though it overlaps with Build a Little Farm and Meadow Story and should not be a new tile until the modes feel meaningfully different.
- **Market Day Team Delivery:** one child chooses an order and the other packs a wagon. This is easy to build, but overlaps strongly with Picnic Partners, so it is not recommended as a separate game unless it gets a substantially different delivery or route mechanic.

## Design checks for future games

- Give each player a clear, changing job that changes what they do.
- Use the full-screen farm scene as the play backdrop and keep controls inside that scene.
- Prefer multiple valid choices for creative and social play. Use gentle retry only when a matching task needs one specific answer.
- Make sound optional and keep the activity playable with device audio muted.
- Stop animation and input callbacks when the game closes, restarts, or returns to the game list.
- Add every new image and sound to the offline cache and check it at phone and tablet sizes.
